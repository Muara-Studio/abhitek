NFC chip, QR code or barcode: which one fits your equipment?

A barcode or a QR code holds a number that can be copied or printed again. An NFC chip has a number from the chip factory, called the UID, that cannot be changed. So one chip stands for one tool only, and an inspection history cannot move to another tool.

When a barcode or a QR code is enough

For items that are cheap, are not safety items and need no one-by-one proof in an audit. Examples are consumables and warehouse stock that is counted by quantity.

When an NFC chip fits better

For equipment whose history must stay with one unit: fall protection, equipment with periodic inspections, and items that change hands. Because the UID cannot be copied, the record of one tool cannot be claimed by another.

How ABHITEK uses it

Each item has an internal code that can be typed and one locked NFC chip. The internal code and the serial number of the maker are kept in two separate fields. The chip is read by an Android phone with NFC or a USB reader; on an iPhone, the record of the item opens with its code.

Questions about chips

Can a chip be moved to another tool?

No. A chip is locked to one tool for good. A damaged chip is replaced with a new one, the reason is recorded, and the number of the old chip goes on the block list.

Which phones can read the chip?

An Android phone with NFC reads the chip straight from the Chrome browser. On an iPhone, the app opens the record of an item when you type its item code. At a desk, a USB reader reads the chip.

Can an item be recorded before its chip is fitted?

Yes. Register the item first with its internal code, then fit the chip at any time. Without a chip, the record of the item opens when you type its code.

Do we have to fit the chips ourselves?

You can, with the fitting guide in the Pilot Pack. Or an ABHITEK technician fits them at your location for Rp10,000 per item, with a minimum of Rp2,000,000 per visit.
